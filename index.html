The Method

Three scores. Never averaged.

Exit readiness is not one question, so it does not get one number. Each leg answers a question the others cannot, and each is scored on its own 0–100 scale from 16 rank-weighted sub-scores. Roll them together and the weakest leg — the one that actually stalls the deal — disappears into the average.

Every score is rule-based and versioned. Nothing is computed by a model, and no score is adjusted after the fact.

A sale-ready business can still fail to close.

A strong DRS with a short Owner Readiness leg stalls just as surely as weak financials do — it simply stalls for a reason nobody wrote down. The three legs have to stand together before a transaction closes on the owner’s terms, which is exactly why they are reported apart.

DRS

Business Readiness

What a buyer’s diligence team will find, and how much of it they will use to recut the price. Transferability: whether the business runs, and keeps its value, without you in it.

BAS

Business Attractiveness

Why a buyer would compete for it rather than merely clear diligence on it. Recurring revenue, growth, differentiation and margin are what move the multiple you negotiate from.

ORI

Owner Readiness

Your own preparedness, personal and financial: the number you actually need after tax, the runway you have, and what you are retiring to. The most overlooked leg, and the one that most often stalls a deal late.

Reading the Scores

Two ladders, because the three scores don’t mean the same thing.

Business Readiness is graded on buyer posture — what a bidder does when they see that number. Attractiveness and Owner Readiness are indices on the same 0–100 scale, so they get a plain readiness ladder instead. Borrowing one set of labels for all three would flatter the two that haven’t earned it.

DRS — Business Readiness

ScoreTierWhat the buyer does
85–100 Institutional Grade Competitive process, multiple bids, seller-favorable terms
70–84 Sale Ready Standard diligence, closes on schedule
55–69 Needs Work Extended diligence, earnouts, escrow, retrade risk on flagged items
40–54 High Risk Distressed pricing, heavy escrow, contingent consideration
< 40 Not Saleable (Yet) No institutional bid. Do not go to market.

Two milestones worth naming: Diligence Ready is a DRS of 70. Competitive Process Ready is 85.

BAS and ORI — Attractiveness and Owner Readiness

ScoreBandWhere it leaves you
90–100 Exit Ready Nothing on this leg is holding the transaction back
70–89 Well Positioned Strong, with specific items still worth closing
50–69 Developing Real progress, not yet aligned with the other legs
30–49 Early Stage This leg is short enough to wobble the plan
< 30 Not Ready Foundational work before a transaction is realistic

How it Works

Four steps from request to roadmap.

01

Profile

Define your exit goals, intended path, priority ranking, and timeline. This shapes how the output is interpreted.

02

Assess

Work through the question bank behind all three legs — 48 sub-scores, 16 to a leg. No financials required.

03

Score

All three scores are computed from your answers by rule — the DRS with its buyer-posture tier, the BAS and ORI with their readiness bands. No model touches a number.

04

Roadmap

The top ranked gaps surface with initiative copy calibrated to severity. An alignment verdict reads all three legs against your stated timeline and names the short one.
